A ghost job is a posting for a role nobody is actively trying to fill. Some companies leave ads up to collect CVs,
some forget to take them down, and some keep a busy careers page because it looks good to investors and customers.
For you it means an evening spent on an application that nobody reads.

We can put a number on it. Every day we check the public job boards of {{ s.companies }} companies that hire
through Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby, the software behind the careers pages of most tech companies and funded
startups. On {{ s.asOf | date: "%-d %B %Y" }} those boards listed {{ s.openJobs }} open jobs.

## Old does not always mean fake

Some roles stay open all year on purpose: sales reps, nurses, drivers, support agents, anything a company hires in
batches. A posting from March for "Account Executive, Mid-Market" may well be real. What should make you suspicious
is one very specific role ("Senior Data Engineer, Payments Platform") that has been up for eight months.

Then there are the honest non-jobs. {{ s.ghost.generalApplications }} of the open postings
({{ s.ghost.generalApplicationsPct }}%) are "general application" or "future opportunities" ads that collect CVs
for no particular role. They at least say so.

## How to check a posting before you apply

1. **Find the date on the company's own careers page.** Job boards like LinkedIn often show "reposted 2 days ago"
   on a job that has been up for months. The company's own page (boards.greenhouse.io, jobs.lever.co,
   jobs.ashbyhq.com) is where the posting really lives.
2. **Search the exact title at that company.** The same title in five cities, or under three slightly different
   names, is often a pipeline ad.
3. **Look for a pay range and a named team.** Someone had to sit down and write those. A vague ad ("various
   locations, competitive pay") costs nothing to leave up.
4. **Apply early.** {{ s.closed.within14Pct }}% of the jobs that closed recently came down within two weeks of going
   up. The first week is your best window.

## How we counted

Postings come from the public job board APIs that companies run for applicants. "Posted" is the date the employer
published the job: Greenhouse's first-published date, Lever's creation date and Ashby's published date. A job
counts as closed when it disappears from its board; we have recorded closures since {{ s.closed.since }}. The
numbers on this page refresh monthly, last on {{ s.asOf }}.
